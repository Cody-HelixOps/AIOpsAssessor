Read this before the call, once you know the number they scored. Each brief is the context for that score: what their results screen already said, what a number like that usually took, and a way into the conversation. It is more than a first reply can hold. Know it, then let the call be a conversation.

Scores 1–3 · Level 1 · Reactive Monitoring

Red result. Firefighting with siloed tools; MTTR runs past a working day. Focus on unified visibility.

Scores 4–7 · Level 2 · Proactive Operations

Amber result. Tool sprawl and alert fatigue; MTTR in hours. Focus on faster RCA.

Scores 8–10 · Level 3 · Preventive Operations

Green result. Reliability-focused and looking at AI agents. Focus on prevention. A 10 says Target reached under the number.

The levels and phase names follow the AIOps Maturity-Based Messaging deck (Sept 2026). Customers see a score out of 10 and their deck level as the title. Say score for the number and Level 1, 2, or 3 only for the phase, so a 2 is never confused with Level 2. The deck’s rule applies: don’t pitch advanced AI to a team still struggling with basic telemetry and correlation. A finished run cannot be a 1 — seven answers are at least 7 points, and 7 points is a score of 2. Bookmark the score you’re prepping, for example sales.html#6.

How the number is calculated

Six questions score 1–4. The AI agents slider scores 1–5. Maximum is 29. The ring shows round(points ÷ 29 × 10), and at least 1. That is the same formula as the results page.

Every question is worth at least 1, so a finished assessment is 7–29 points and lands on scores 2–10. A score of 1 only exists if the run didn’t finish. Scores 1–3 are Level 1, 4–7 are Level 2, and 8–10 are Level 3.
